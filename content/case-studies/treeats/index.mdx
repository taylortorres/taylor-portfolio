<Section id="challenge" title="Challenge" label="The Challenge" headline="Choosing where to eat on campus feels like a gamble">

It's 6:30 PM, and you're a college student juggling classes, clubs, and a looming deadline. But when it comes to dinner... where do you even start? Choosing where to eat on campus feels like a gamble.

<PostPanel
  src={fizzPost}
  alt="A Fizz post from an anonymous Stanford student: “One day, I wanna live in a world where I can go to any dining hall and not sigh,” with 58 upvotes"
  sigh
/>

I read through Fizz, the app 95% of Stanford students use, and looked at the two existing dining sites. The same questions kept coming up: what's being served, when the specials are, and what's in the food. The official site was outdated, and the student-run one had no real-time busyness, so the answers weren't there.

<Callout label="How might we...">design a dining experience that saves time, fosters community, and *leaves students feeling satisfied with their choices*?</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="solution" title="Solution" label="The Solution" headline="Treeats turns students into each other’s dining guide">

Treeats is a campus dining app where students share real-time busyness, browse menus, and earn Acorn Points for keeping each other informed.

<Section id="digging-deeper" title="Digging Deeper" headline="Why wasn’t anyone contributing? ( ರ_•́)">

Stanfood was the closest attempt at delivering student-driven dining info with student-taken photos, ratings, and a social tab. But **reviews were sparse, and the social tab was mostly its founder posting on his own (-‿-")**.

That made me wonder: If students cared this much about dining info, ***why weren’t they pitching in?***

<BodyLabel>so, **i interviewed 6 students**. this is what i heard.</BodyLabel>

- Hard to tell what's good on any given day
- Always in a rush, just want to eat in peace
- Like seeing where friends are

<Callout label="Student quote">“They had sushi at Wilbur last week AND halo halo, which is my favorite, and I had no idea. I saw people post about it afterwards.”</Callout>

<Insight label="Key insight">While students value a platform that combines essential dining information with a sense of community, their busy schedules mean ***they’re unlikely to engage without clear incentives.***</Insight>

### Gamifying engagement with campus spirit

I incorporated gamification into the app by **creating an acorn mascot**, drawing inspiration from the **many squirrels on Stanford’s campus** (after all, acorns are their favorite treat!). This addition aimed to enhance the campus experience with a playful, cozy touch, motivating students to engage with the app and **earn Acorn Points and custom dining badges as rewards**.

<Section id="design-evolution" title="Design Evolution" headline="Testing with students pushed me to rethink busyness, accessibility, and rewards">

After testing the mid-fidelity prototypes with students, **I implemented three key changes based on their feedback**, transforming the design into its final high-fidelity version.

<EvolutionStep
  n={1}
  title="Busyness had to show more than wait time"
  before={{ src: busynessBefore, w: 82.54, y: 53.6, frame: "shadow", alt: "Before: a Wait Times tab reading “Live: Busier than usual,” with a bar graph of the day’s wait times from 8a to 8p and the current hour in red" }}
  after={{ src: busynessAfter, w: 89.92, frame: "border-shadow", alt: "After: a Busyness tab where a sleepy acorn mascot sits beside “Live: Busy (Long wait, no seating)” and a Help Update Status button" }}
  feedback={["Wait time alone missed seating in buffet-style halls", "The bar graph was hard to read without numbers"]}
  change={["Merged wait time and seating into one status", "Swapped the bar graph for a live mood-changing mascot that indicates status", "Made “Help Update Status” more prominent"]}
/>

<EvolutionStep
  n={2}
  title="Ensuring accessibility standards are met"
  before={{ src: accessibilityBefore, w: 62, y: 54.1, frame: "shadow", caption: <><strong>Failed</strong> all WCAG accessibility standards</>, alt: "Before: a contrast check of white “Handgloves” text on the light green #4BAA8A, a 2.83:1 ratio that fails for normal text, large text and graphics" }}
  after={{ src: accessibilityAfter, w: 62, y: 54.1, frame: "shadow", caption: <><strong>Passed</strong> all WCAG accessibility standards</>, alt: "After: the same check on the darker green #00615F, a 7.3:1 ratio that passes AA and AAA for normal and large text, and AA for graphics" }}
  feedback="Hard to read certain text and graphic-heavy screens."
  change="Darkened the primary color, raising contrast from 2.83:1 to 7.3:1. It now passes all WCAG standards."
/>

<EvolutionStep
  n={3}
  title="Students wanted real rewards, not just badges"
  before={{ src: badgesBefore, w: 85, frame: "border", alt: "Before: a My Badges row with Sweet Treat Fanatic, Cultural Feastmaster and Caffeinated Connoisseur badges" }}
  after={{ src: badgesAfter, w: 77.4, frame: "border", alt: "After: a 25-day streak and 275 Acorn Points above a Leaderboard Progress bar at 85%, with “Keep nibbling! Just 15 more points to join the Top 10!”" }}
  feedback="Many students reported wanting tangible rewards over levels and badges."
  change="Added a quarterly leaderboard where the top 10 contributors earn dining dollars, with Acorn Points tracking progress."
